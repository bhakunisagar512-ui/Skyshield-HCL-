# SOP-WIL: Wildlife (bird and animal) strike response
Code: SOP-WIL
Category: wildlife_strike
Source: SkyShield sample SOP (synthetic, for development). Themes follow DGCA NASP 2024-2028, N-HRC 4 Wildlife strikes. Not official DGCA text.

## 1. Scope
Applies to any confirmed or suspected strike of birds or animals on the aircraft during take-off, climb, approach, landing or taxi, including strikes found during walk-around inspection.

## 2. Hazard background
Most strikes happen below 3,000 ft near airports. Engine ingestion can cause power loss, vibration or fan blade damage. Strikes on the windshield, radome, pitot probes or landing gear can affect instruments and structure.

## 3. Immediate measures
- MUST: Carry out a bird strike inspection of engines, radome, windshield, leading edges and landing gear before the next flight, as per the aircraft maintenance manual.
- MUST: Perform a borescope inspection of the affected engine if ingestion is suspected or engine parameters showed abnormal vibration or exhaust gas temperature.
- MUST: Ground the aircraft until the inspection is signed off by a licensed engineer.
- SHOULD: Collect remains (feathers, tissue) for species identification.

## 4. Airport coordination
- MUST: Inform the airport operator's wildlife hazard management unit with time, runway and location of the strike.
- SHOULD: Request a runway inspection for carcasses after the strike.
- SHOULD: Review garbage disposal, grass height and water bodies around the runway strip with the airport operator.

## 5. Crew awareness
- SHOULD: Brief crews operating at the same airport about the bird activity, especially at dawn and dusk.
- SHOULD: Use landing lights during take-off and approach below 10,000 ft to make the aircraft more visible to birds.
